# Optical Technology for Data Centres

How light carries data-centre and AI-cluster traffic, and what it costs: pluggable modules and the linear-drive optics that remove their DSPs, co-packaged optics on the switch package, optical circuit switches that replace packet spines, and the scale-up and scale-out networks of AI clusters. Device physics comes from [Silicon Photonics Fundamentals](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/Optical_Hub_Silicon_Photonics) and is linked, not repeated.

**Live index:** https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Optical_Hub_Data_Centres/

## Where the numbers come from

This is mostly a literature series. Every number is cited on its slide to a paper, a standard (IEEE 802.3, OIF, ITU-T), an MSA or a datasheet, with a link, or computed live by a deck's interactive:

- **Deck 01** splits a module's power into DSP, optics and other parts for retimed, LRO and LPO modules and scales it to a switch. Its defaults are **illustrative**, chosen to sit inside the cited vendor ranges.
- **Deck 02** adds energy per bit along the electrical path for DSP pluggables, linear-drive pluggables and co-packaged optics. Its defaults are **illustrative**, consistent with deck 01.
- **Deck 03** computes an optical circuit switch's duty cycle from its reconfiguration time, with presets taken from cited sources, and the power of replacing a packet spine. The OCS power per port is computed from Google's published Palomar figures; the packet-port power is **illustrative**.
- **Deck 04** counts switches, links and transceivers in a non-blocking fat-tree and their power; module powers are cited presets, and switch and accelerator powers are **illustrative**.

Much of what is public about data-centre optics comes from vendors. Their press releases and datasheets are cited and labelled as vendor claims. Standards in progress (IEEE P802.3dj) are called drafts.

## Glossary: concepts and where they are explained

The same glossary, with anchors each deck links to, is on the [live index](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Optical_Hub_Data_Centres/#glossary).

| Concept | What it is | Where it is explained |
|---|---|---|
| Pluggable transceiver and MSA | A self-contained optical link end that slides into a faceplate cage: electrical lanes on one side, fibre on the other. A multi-source agreement fixes the cage, connector, management interface and power envelope, so switches and optics come from different suppliers and failed modules are swapped per port. | [OptDC 01 · what a pluggable module is](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-01) |
| QSFP-DD and OSFP | The two eight-lane form factors of 400G and 800G pluggables. QSFP-DD's power rating rose from 20 W (2019) to 25 W (2021); OSFP has an integrated heat sink for up to 15 W with conventional airflow; OSFP-XD doubles the lanes to 16. | [OptDC 01 · what a pluggable module is](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-01); [OptDC 01 · faceplates, heat and the switch](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-11) |
| Retiming DSP | The digital signal processor in a module that recovers clock and data from the lossy host trace and re-times them for the optics, and the reverse on receive. It splits one hard link into two separately specified ones, at a cost of several watts per module. | [OptDC 01 · inside a DSP-retimed module](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-02); [OptDC 01 · where a module's power goes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-05) |
| Ethernet optical interfaces: SR, DR, FR, LR | The IEEE 802.3 and MSA optical interfaces. SR: multimode VCSELs, about 100 m. DR: one wavelength per single-mode fibre pair, 500 m. FR and LR: four or eight wavelengths on a duplex fibre, 2 and 10 km. 200 Gb/s-per-lane versions are in the IEEE P802.3dj draft. | [OptDC 01 · the optical interfaces](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-03); [OptDC 01 · the reach ladder](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-10) |
| Host electrical interface (VSR) | The channel from a switch's SerDes to a pluggable module: package, board trace and connector. OIF's CEI-112G-VSR allows up to 16 dB of loss at the Nyquist frequency, including one connector, at 100 Gb/s per lane. | [OptDC 01 · the host electrical interface](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-04); [OptDC 02 · why move the optics into the package](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-01) |
| Module power | What a pluggable draws: an 800G datasheet gives 17 W maximum; DSP vendors claim 800G modules below 11–12 W. At 800 Gb/s, 11–17 W is about 14–21 pJ/bit, most of it DSP, drivers, TIAs and lasers. | [OptDC 01 · where a module's power goes](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-05); [OptDC 01 · interactive module power](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-08); [OptDC 04 · interactive transceiver count](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-06) |
| Linear-drive pluggable optics (LPO) | A pluggable with no DSP: linear drivers and TIAs pass the waveform through, and the switch's SerDes equalises the whole link. Vendors claim roughly half the module power; the LPO MSA (formed 2024) released its first specification, for 100 Gb/s-per-lane single-mode modules, in 2025. | [OptDC 01 · linear-drive pluggable optics](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-06); [OptDC 01 · interactive module power](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-08); [OptDC 01 · what linear drive gives up](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-09) |
| Linear receive optics (LRO) | A half-way module: retimed transmit, linear receive. The launched optical signal stays standard and interoperable while the receive side goes linear; described by one vendor as saving roughly half as much as LPO. | [OptDC 01 · linear receive](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-07) |
| Copper reach | How far passive twin-axial copper carries a lane: at least 2 m at 100 Gb/s per lane (IEEE 802.3ck) and an objective of at least 1 m at 200 Gb/s (P802.3dj). It roughly halves with each lane-rate doubling, which pushes optics towards the chip. | [OptDC 01 · the reach ladder](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-10); [OptDC 04 · copper against optics](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-08) |
| Switch capacity and radix | A switch chip's total bandwidth and its number of ports: 51.2 Tb/s is 64 × 800G, 102.4 Tb/s is 128 × 800G or 64 × 1.6T. Radix sets how many endpoints each tier of a fat-tree can join. | [OptDC 01 · faceplates, heat and the switch](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_01_Pluggables_to_LPO/#slide-11); [OptDC 04 · fat-trees, rails and rail-only](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-05); [OptDC 04 · interactive transceiver count](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-06) |
| Co-packaged optics (CPO) | Optical engines on the switch or processor package substrate, millimetres from the die, instead of pluggable modules at the faceplate. It removes the host trace and the module DSP; switch vendors claim 50–70% lower optical interconnect power, at the cost of field replacement of optics. | [OptDC 02 · why move the optics into the package](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-01); [OptDC 02 · anatomy of a co-packaged switch](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-02); [OptDC 02 · what vendors have announced](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-04) |
| Optical engine | The silicon photonic chip, with its drivers and TIAs, that converts a group of electrical lanes to light on the package: for example 6.4 Tb/s per engine, eight around a 51.2 Tb/s switch. | [OptDC 02 · anatomy of a co-packaged switch](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-02) |
| XSR and XSR+ electrical lanes | OIF's extra-short-reach lanes from a die to another die or to an optical engine: CEI-112G-XSR reaches up to 50 mm; XSR+ allows up to 13 dB bump to bump, for multi-chip modules, CPO and near-packaged optics. | [OptDC 02 · why move the optics into the package](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-01); [OptDC 02 · the OIF agreements](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-03); [OptDC 02 · interactive energy along the path](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-05) |
| OIF co-packaging agreements | The Co-Packaging Framework (2022), the 3.2 Tb/s co-packaged module (2023: 8 × 400G FR4 or DR4 optics behind 32 XSR lanes), the external laser module ELSFP (2023, revised 2025) and the XSR+ lane (2024). | [OptDC 02 · the OIF agreements](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-03) |
| Optical I/O chiplet | An optical engine inside a processor package, for scale-up and memory traffic, typically with many wavelengths at modest rates per wavelength: Intel's prototype runs 64 lanes at 32 Gb/s on 8 wavelengths per fibre; vendors claim about 5 pJ/bit. | [OptDC 02 · optical I/O for compute](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-06); [OptDC 04 · scale-up domains](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-03) |
| Near-packaged optics | Optical engines on the board next to the package rather than on it: a shorter electrical channel than pluggables, with some separation of optics and switch. One step on the path from faceplate to die. | [OptDC 02 · near-packaged optics](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-10) |
| Serviceability of co-packaged optics | What CPO gives up and how designs recover it: lasers kept in front-panel pluggable modules, known-good engines before assembly, spare lanes, and detachable fibre connectors. | [OptDC 02 · serviceability and reliability](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-07); [OptDC 02 · fibre at scale](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-09) |
| Fibre count | Data fibres needed for a given bandwidth: lanes ÷ wavelengths per fibre, doubled for two directions. 51.2 Tb/s of 100G lanes needs 1,024 fibres with one lane per fibre, 256 with four wavelengths per fibre, plus laser feeds. | [OptDC 02 · fibre at scale](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-09) |
| Optical circuit switch (OCS) | A switch that steers light from input to output fibres without detecting it: no transceivers inside, under 1 W per port for Google's Palomar, and agnostic to line rate. It sets circuits, not packets, and reconfigures in nanoseconds to milliseconds depending on technology. | [OptDC 03 · packet and circuit switching](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-01); [OptDC 03 · how a MEMS OCS works](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-02); [OptDC 03 · the technologies compared](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-03) |
| 3D MEMS optical switch | Two arrays of two-axis micro-mirrors that aim each input beam at any output fibre. Hundreds of ports at under 2 dB typical loss, switching in milliseconds: Google's Palomar is 136 × 136. | [OptDC 03 · how a MEMS OCS works](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-02); [OptDC 03 · the technologies compared](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-03) |
| Reconfiguration time and duty cycle | How long a circuit switch takes to change its connections, against how long a configuration is used: the fabric carries traffic for 1 − treconfigure/tperiod of the time. Milliseconds suit per-job topologies; per-packet switching needs nanoseconds. | [OptDC 03 · interactive reconfiguration](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-07); [OptDC 03 · reconfiguration time against traffic](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-08) |
| Direct connect and topology engineering | Wiring aggregation blocks to each other through an OCS layer instead of through a packet spine, and choosing that wiring to match the traffic matrix. Google reports 30% lower capital cost and 41% lower power for its Jupiter fabric. | [OptDC 03 · Google's optical spine](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-04) |
| Optical circulator | A non-reciprocal device that lets one fibre carry both directions of a link, halving OCS ports and fibres; it makes the switch's return loss matter, since reflections reach the same port's receiver. | [OptDC 03 · lightwave fabrics](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-05) |
| Optically reconfigurable torus (TPU v4) | 4,096 TPU v4 chips in 64-chip cubes joined through 3D MEMS OCSes into tori of the shape each job asks for, routing around failed racks; the OCSes are under 5% of system cost and 3% of power. | [OptDC 03 · TPU v4](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-06); [OptDC 04 · tori and optical switches](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-07) |
| Fast optical switching (RotorNet, Sirius) | Research designs that switch light far faster than MEMS: RotorNet cycles rotor switches through fixed matchings with no central scheduler; Sirius uses tunable lasers and passive gratings to reconfigure end to end in 3.84 ns. | [OptDC 03 · reconfiguration time against traffic](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-08); [OptDC 03 · the technologies compared](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-03) |
| OCS insertion loss in the link budget | The loss every link pays to cross an optical switch: typically under 2 dB for MEMS switches, several dB for integrated silicon switches. With no regeneration inside, it comes straight out of the transceivers' margin. | [OptDC 03 · an optical switch in the link budget](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-09); [OptDC 03 · interactive reconfiguration](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_03_Optical_Circuit_Switching/#slide-07) |
| Scale-up domain | Accelerators joined by a very high-bandwidth, low-latency fabric with memory semantics: NVLink (NVL72: 72 GPUs, 130 TB/s), UALink (up to 1,024 accelerators at 200 Gb/s per lane), Google's ICI. Mostly copper inside a rack today. | [OptDC 04 · scale-up, scale-out and scale-across](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-01); [OptDC 04 · scale-up domains](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-03) |
| Scale-out fabric | The NIC-based network joining thousands to hundreds of thousands of accelerators: InfiniBand or Ethernet (RoCE, Ultra Ethernet), built as a non-blocking fat-tree, mostly optical. | [OptDC 04 · scale-up, scale-out and scale-across](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-01); [OptDC 04 · scale-out fabrics in practice](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-04) |
| Fat-tree (folded Clos) | A non-blocking network of identical switches in tiers; with k-port switches, three tiers join up to k3/4 endpoints (65,536 at radix 64). Every tier boundary carries one link per endpoint, so a t-tier tree needs 2·E·t transceivers if all links are optical. | [OptDC 04 · fat-trees, rails and rail-only](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-05); [OptDC 04 · interactive transceiver count](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-06) |
| Rail-optimised and rail-only networks | Rail-optimised: GPU i of every server connects to rail switch i, so same-numbered GPUs are one hop apart. Rail-only removes the spine and forwards the little cross-rail traffic through the scale-up domain, reporting 38–77% lower network cost and 37–75% lower power. | [OptDC 04 · fat-trees, rails and rail-only](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-05); [OptDC 04 · pluggable, linear or co-packaged](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-09) |
| Torus interconnect | Accelerators linked directly to their neighbours in two or three dimensions with no switches between: cheap per chip and good for ring and neighbour patterns, poorer for all-to-all and sensitive to failed nodes unless optical switches reroute. | [OptDC 04 · tori and optical switches](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-07) |
| Transceiver count of a cluster | Two per optical link: 2 × endpoints × tiers for a non-blocking fat-tree, about 98,000 for 16,384 accelerators in three tiers. Topology (rail-only, OCS, copper) sets the count; module type (DSP, LPO, CPO) sets the watts each. | [OptDC 04 · interactive transceiver count](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-06); [OptDC 04 · pluggable, linear or co-packaged](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-09) |
| Link failures in training | A synchronous training job stops when any participant's link fails. In Llama 3's 54-day snapshot on 16,384 GPUs, 35 of 419 unexpected interruptions (8.4%) were network switches or cables; at a trade-press 200 FIT per transceiver, 100,000 transceivers fail about once every two days. | [OptDC 04 · how links fail](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-10); [OptDC 02 · serviceability and reliability](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_02_Co_Packaged_Optics/#slide-07) |
| RoCE and Ultra Ethernet | Ethernet for AI fabrics: RDMA over Converged Ethernet carries GPU-to-GPU transfers on Ethernet switches (Meta's 24,576-GPU cluster); the Ultra Ethernet Consortium's 1.0 specification (2025) defines a new transport and congestion control for AI and HPC. | [OptDC 04 · scale-out fabrics in practice](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-04) |
| Scale-across | Training one model across several data centres over long-haul coherent links, with algorithms that synchronise rarely (DiLoCo: 500 times less communication on its benchmark). | [OptDC 04 · scale-up, scale-out and scale-across](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-01); [OptDC 04 · scale-across](https://brendanjameslynskey.github.io/OptDC_04_AI_Cluster_Networks/#slide-11) |
